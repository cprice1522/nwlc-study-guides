  1. Circuit voltage
  2. Service voltage
  3. Transformer voltage rating
  4. kVA ratings
  5. Polarity
  6. Impedance

kVA Rating Rules

Secondary TypekVA Sizing Rule
Wye SecondaryAll three transformers equal size — single-phase load shared equally
Delta Secondary (combo loads)Usually one larger transformer — carries 2/3 of single-phase load and 1/3 of three-phase load

Polarity Rules

PolarityStandardPositive Terminals
Additive200 kVA and smaller, primary 8,660V and belowH-1 and X-1
SubtractiveAll other transformersH-1 and X-1

Using different polarity transformers will work if connected correctly but causes confusion and safety concerns for future crews. A reversed polarity transformer in a bank causes extremely high current flow when energized.

In a Wye connection: negative polarity terminals (H-2, X-3) connect together; positive terminals (H-1, X-1) connect to phases.
In a Delta connection: positive terminal always connects to negative terminal, then to the phase.

Open Banks — Key Rules

  • Open bank = two single-phase transformers banked together; one leg of the Delta or Wye is "open"
  • Open banks are 57.7% as efficient as closed (three-transformer) banks
  • Used when single-phase load is larger than three-phase load
  • Three-phase power CANNOT be derived from an open bank if the secondary is connected Open Wye
  • Secondary is always Open Delta when delivering three-phase power from an open bank
  • Primary can be Open Delta OR Open Wye depending on system voltage and nameplate
  • If one transformer of a three-unit Delta secondary bank fails, the bank can be temporarily re-wired as an open bank

Impedance Mismatch Warning

Mismatched impedances between paralleled or banked transformers require a de-rating because the load does not divide in proportion to the kVA ratings. The lowest impedance transformer supplies the greatest share of the load and may become overloaded.

⚠ Every effort should be made to select transformers with the same impedances when paralleling or banking.
