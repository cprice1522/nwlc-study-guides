Apply the primary connection rule. Which connection is correct?

Answer: Cannot be connected — The nameplate shows a single number "2,400" which is a Delta-only transformer. However, on a 2,400/4,160 system, 2,400V is the phase-to-ground voltage — not the phase-to-phase voltage. A Delta transformer cannot be connected phase-to-ground. This nameplate cannot be used on this system.

Wye-Wye Banks

  • Each transformer connected between a phase and grounded neutral
  • Each provides ONE hot leg on the secondary for three-phase power
  • Three-phase power requires all three transformers — open Wye secondary cannot produce three-phase
  • Secondary coils paralleled; X3 lug is idle; paralleling done between X1 and X2
  • Advantage: All three transformers share single-phase load equally; can use single or dual bushing transformers
  • Disadvantage: If one transformer fails, customer is down to one phase
  • Typical voltages (120V coil): n-h = 120V, h-h = 208V
  • Typical voltages (277V coil): n-h = 277V, h-h = 480V

Wye-Delta Banks

  • Primary: Wye point is FLOATED (not grounded) — must use two-bushing transformers
  • Grounding the Wye point creates a ground source — could cause severe transformer overloading
  • The floating Wye point is a hazard — it is NOT grounded
  • Can be used on both three- and four-wire systems
  • Secondary coils connected in series
  • Power leg (high leg, wild leg, stinger leg): 1.73× greater than neutral-to-hot voltage — has no use electrically; is a by-product of the Delta secondary
  • Three-phase motors only see h-h voltage and do not "notice" the power leg
  • Lighter transformer: the one with X2 grounded as neutral — carries majority of single-phase load — LARGER kVA
  • Typical 120/240V: n-p = 208V, n-h = 120V, h-h & h-p = 240V
  • Typical 240/480V: n-p = 416V, n-h = 240V, h-h & h-p = 480V

Delta-Wye Banks

  • Primary: H2s connected to H1s creating a symmetrical pattern, connected to each phase
  • Primary Delta suitable for both three- and four-wire systems
  • Secondary: same Wye connection as Wye-Wye bank; X2 bushings from all three tie together to neutral
  • X3 lug is idle; coils paralleled between X1 and X2
  • Typical (120V coil): n-h = 120V, h-h = 208V
  • Typical (277V coil): n-h = 277V, h-h = 480V

Delta-Delta Banks

  • Primary suitable for three- or four-wire systems
  • Secondary coils connected in series; power leg present
  • Lighter transformer: X2 grounded as neutral — carries majority of lighting load — LARGER kVA
  • Power transformers: the other two — share equal portions of single-phase and three-phase load — SMALLER kVA
  • With one unit out: can be reconnected as open Delta-open Delta (57.7% of original rating)
  • Typical 120/240V: n-p = 208V, n-h = 120V, h-h & h-p = 240V
  • Typical 240/480V: n-p = 416V, n-h = 240V, h-h & h-p = 480V

Underground Three-Phase Transformers

  • Three-phase transformers used — coils banked internally at the factory
  • Lineworker must rely entirely on the nameplate to determine connections
  • Open Wye-open Delta banks can be installed using two single-phase padmount transformers; banking performed with secondary cables through short ducts
  • Power transformer in underground open bank: grounding strap must be removed from X2
